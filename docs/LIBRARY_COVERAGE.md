## What “complete” must mean

The useful target is a **comprehensive nursing reference with a defined coverage checklist**, starting with adult ICU and routine inpatient care. “Everything in medicine” has no testable finish line and obscures nursing workflows. Pediatric, neonatal, maternity, and specialty material needs its own population and scope labels; adult reference values must not silently serve as pediatric guidance.

The first complete ICU/bedside release needs a reviewed reference path for each of these work areas:

1. Assessment and trends: vital signs, focused assessments, pain, cognition, neurovascular checks, deterioration recognition, and escalation.
2. Critical care: oxygen delivery, ventilation, blood gases, shock/hemodynamic monitoring, rhythms, sedation, delirium, neurocritical care, renal replacement, and recovery/mobility.
3. Medication reference: canonical drug monographs, administration considerations, monitoring, adverse effects, high-alert risks, interactions, and linked infusion references. Patient-specific prescribing remains outside scope.
4. Labs and diagnostic tests: specimen requirements, units, reference-population context, limitations, interpretation, and factors that affect results.
5. Procedures and devices: purpose, preparation, nursing role, monitoring, complications, and maintenance. Local-policy/device-specific steps must be identified as such.
6. Everyday care: nutrition, swallowing/aspiration precautions, elimination, hygiene, skin integrity, mobility, falls, pain, sleep, and infection prevention.
7. Conditions by body system: concise nursing assessment and monitoring context, linked tests and procedures, complications, and source-backed education.
8. Communication and professional practice: handoffs, escalation, delegation, documentation, consent, incident reporting, interpreter use, privacy, and teaching.
9. Care transitions: admission, transfer, discharge, home-care education, goals of care, palliative care, and end-of-life support.
10. Specialty extensions: emergency, perioperative, obstetric, pediatric, neonatal, oncology, behavioral health, gerontology, dialysis, rehabilitation, and community care. Extend deliberately after the ICU/bedside checklist is met.

This is a product coverage checklist, not a clinical practice guideline. Existing entries establish **presence**, not sufficient depth or correctness.

## Publication gate for a coverage item

Track these states separately: **identified → source mapped → drafted/consolidated → source verified → clinical review complete where required → published → monitored**. A count or an existing title cannot skip a state.

Every item needs:

- One canonical entry ID, aliases, population, care setting, topic memberships, and a clearly defined nursing scope.
- Source-level commercial-use and attribution verification. Use `CONTENT_SOURCING.md`; do not ingest competitor content or infer permission from public accessibility.
- A short reference view and a deeper view where appropriate, both traceable to the source. Include assessment/monitoring context and limitations rather than unsupported bedside instructions.
- Claim-level citation support, including numerical tables, units, and population-specific ranges. Citation IDs resolving is only the structural check; reviewers must verify that the linked passage supports the claim.
- Source version/date and a documented review date. Source-fidelity review and credentialed clinical review must have distinct labels.
- The clinical review required by the current engineering brief: Tier A pre-publication review for high-alert drugs, pediatric dosing, and emergency protocols; applicable Tier B sampling; an operational reporting loop.
- A working report-an-issue path, correction owner, and an update/retirement procedure.
- Successful search by common name and abbreviation, a reachable topic/category path, correct related links, and readable offline content.

Do not mark any area “complete” until its explicit subtopic checklist and review gates are met. The older curator-model notes in `PLAN.md`/`v1_SCOPE.md` conflict with the current engineering brief about clinical review; this coverage plan follows the current brief and does not waive review.
