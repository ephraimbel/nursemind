# Respiratory, vascular and hematology source review — September 17, 2026

Ten adult nursing guides add 67 sections, 134 cited content blocks and 4,168 body words from 19 primary NIH source pages. The manifest maps each block to its source and records scope, topic placement and related references. Extracted source snapshots with URL/retrieval/hash metadata are retained for all 19 sources; saved-text hashes were verified. Raw HTML is not retained.

## Sourcing and scope

NHLBI federal prose and NLM-authored MedlinePlus Medical Tests body text support the adaptations. MedlinePlus's reuse policy explicitly includes Medical Tests information in its public-domain content: https://medlineplus.gov/about/using/usingcontent/. Linked commercial references, drug pages, A.D.A.M. encyclopedia text, images, videos and illustrations are not ingested into the guides. Workflow prompts organize the source-supported education; they are not claimed as independently validated procedure protocols.

The sources support patient preparation, symptom observations, basic distinctions among tests, and continuity of care. Existing bronchoscopy/thoracentesis procedure pages and disease summaries retain their separate identifiers and scope. These new guides focus on preparation and follow-up, with links back to those entries. No procedural credential, patient-specific prescription, diagnostic interpretation or complete emergency protocol is supplied.

## Fidelity checks and exclusions

- Bronchoscopy/BAL: lavage fluid and tissue biopsy are distinguished. Oral intake waits for numbing medicine to wear off and the procedural recovery criteria. Sedation requires the individual discharge/escort plan. Lab and tissue results need follow-up after procedural recovery.
- Thoracentesis: source-backed positioning, discomfort/cough reporting, fluid testing and complications are included. There is no universal drainage volume, drainage rate, needle-placement instruction or mandatory-imaging rule. Severe chest or breathing symptoms require urgent assessment.
- Pulmonary function: preparation and patient experience are separated for spirometry, lung volumes, diffusion and exercise testing. The NLM source incorrectly describes carbon dioxide measurement in its diffusion-test paragraph; that sentence is excluded. No numerical predicted-value equation, independent inhaler hold, or automatic diagnosis from one result is added. The different denture instructions for pulmonary testing and bronchoscopy are preserved.
- Pleural effusion: fluid, inflammation and air are distinguished. Fluid studies support cause-specific care, and drainage does not replace treatment of the cause. The Nucleus Medical Media illustration is excluded. Pneumothorax-specific travel restrictions are not applied to all effusions. Fixed catheter schedules and manipulation instructions are excluded.
- VTE recovery: recurrent symptoms, bleeding, post-thrombotic symptoms and cardiopulmonary follow-up are included. There is no universal anticoagulation duration, dose adjustment or aspirin substitution. Monitoring is medicine-specific; vitamin K consistency is described for warfarin rather than as a rule for every anticoagulant.
- PAD: daily foot inspection, protective footwear, professional foot care, infection reporting and acute limb changes are distinguished. The older source incorrectly groups pneumococcal vaccination with yearly vaccination; that statement is excluded. No debridement or compression prescription is inferred.
- ITP: symptom and platelet-count review remain linked; drug regimens and routes are omitted, including the source's erroneous oral-route grouping of romiplostim. OTC bleeding risks are distinguished from an instruction to stop prescribed antiplatelet therapy. Splenectomy needs individualized infection-prevention follow-up.
- Aplastic anemia: marrow failure is not reduced to red-cell symptoms alone; infection, bleeding, blood/marrow tests, cause review and treatment continuity are covered. No neutropenia threshold, transfusion trigger or prophylaxis regimen is invented.
- Hemolytic anemia: the mechanism is separated from the cause; history, symptoms, testing and response to cause-specific treatment are addressed. A transfusion reaction is not diagnosed by this page, and no treatment algorithm is reproduced.
- B12 deficiency: neurologic symptoms, intake versus absorption, medication history, cause-specific replacement and prolonged recovery are covered. The source's inconsistent numerical diagnostic tables, intake table and pediatric claims are excluded. No universal laboratory cutoff or replacement dose is supplied.
- General anemia testing: CBC and cause-focused investigations are retained. The source's broad claim that high hemoglobin implies anemia and its population-specific numerical tables are not reproduced.

All ten guides conservatively remain pending independent Tier A clinical review. Source-fidelity dates record source review, not clinical approval. The manifest is review metadata rather than a runtime publication gate. No release or production publication occurred; older linked pages retain their prior review status.
